### What both cards do today

1. A typed target **constrains the reachable range**, and the readout shows it.
2. A **lock narrows that range** — 5.86–41.99 became 17.90–24.48. Measured, and
   it is the half of Jon's expectation that already holds.
3. **Apply** solves the unlocked shares to hit the target, exactly.
4. **A drag afterwards abandons the target**, rebalancing to sum 100 with no
   reference to the blend. The held share is still exact (33.333333), so the
   padlock is honoured; the target is not.

### What the settled entry specifies

From `SETTLED: constrained mix mode, not input-vs-target`:

> **Moving one slider rebalances the untouched ones deterministically.** With
> two sliders flexing, the outcome is *exactly determined* by the sum and blend
> equations — there is no choice to make and therefore no recommendation to
> offer.

**"the sum AND BLEND equations."** The entry specifies that a drag under a
target solves the others against BOTH — which is precisely the behaviour Jon
expected and neither card has. The implementation satisfies the sum equation
only.

A second line in the same entry points the same way:

> **An unreachable target is flagged BEFORE interaction**, not discovered by
> dragging into a wall.

That sentence presupposes that dragging can reach a wall. Today it cannot,
because the target stops constraining the moment a slider moves — there is no
wall to hit, which is a stronger divergence than the first line alone shows.

### So this is not a parity gap

D4-01 was briefed as a possible parity gap under decision 7. It is not: the two
cards are identical, to six decimals, through one shared function. It is a
**specification-versus-implementation gap on both cards at once**, and the
brief reserves it: *"Holding the target under drag is then a product decision
for Jon, not this session's."*

### What a fix would touch, stated so the decision can be costed

Not a recommendation — the decision is reserved. But the shape is measurable:

- `rebalance` would need the per-member rates and the target, or a new
  sibling in `mixConstraint.ts` would; the panel "computes nothing" and should
  stay that way, so the engine is the right home.
- Both drag handlers (`:1968`, `:2219`) would call it, which is one call site
  each and no new duplication.
- The **three-band value axis is exactly determined** with one tier held (the
  entry's own design note), so on that axis there is no freedom to design away.
  The multi-slider tariff axis is where it would need real thought, and the same
  note already flags that as the second half of the feature.
- **What happens when the drag would leave the reachable range is genuinely
  undecided.** Today there is no such case, because the target is abandoned
  rather than enforced. The entry says an unreachable target is flagged before
  interaction, but says nothing about a drag that would make a reachable target
  unreachable. There is no existing behaviour to mirror, which is exactly why
  the brief's "do not invent a new refusal" cannot be satisfied without a
  decision.

That last point is the one that most needs Jon: it is not implementation detail,
it is a missing clause in the settled entry.
