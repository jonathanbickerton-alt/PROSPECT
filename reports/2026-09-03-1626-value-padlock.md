## Item 3 — persistence, both carriers

**The Value card's mix rides on `YieldEvent`** (`tariffMix`) — measured, not
assumed. So there are two carriers and two columns.

| carrier | mix field | new column | written | read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `MarketEvent` (promotions) | `promoMix` | `Promo_Mix_Locked` | `marketEventExportRow` | `readStoredEventModifiers` |
| `YieldEvent` (Value card) | `tariffMix` | `Tariff_Mix_Locked` | `yieldEventExportRow` | `yieldEventFromRow` |

**One field name across both: `mixLocked?: string[]`.** It is one concept with
two homes, and naming it twice is how two things that must agree begin to
diverge.

**Pinned-order position: APPENDED, immediately after the mix column it belongs
to** — `Promo_Mix_Locked` after `Promo_Mix_JSON`, `Tariff_Mix_Locked` after
`Tariff_Mix_JSON`. Both positions are asserted in the spec rather than described.
A reader keying by column NAME is indifferent, but the sheet's column order is
what a human diffing two exports reads, and inserting would shift everything
after it.

**Absence is the empty string, never `[]`.** An empty array would claim "a lock
set with no members", which round-trips as a different statement from "no
locks" — the same rule `Promo_Mix_JSON` and the band-override map already
follow. Asserted on the way out as well as in.

**Restore and re-edit**: `handleEditPromoStart` and `handleEditYieldStart` seed
their lock state from the event, so reopening shows the padlocks it was saved
with. An event without the field restores with none rather than inheriting
whatever the card last held.

**A workbook without the column loads clean** — and so do four malformed forms
(empty string, malformed JSON, an empty array, a non-array), each asserted to
read as absence. **A phantom lock is worse than no lock**: a padlock the user
did not set, cannot explain, and can only discover by finding a slider that
will not move.
