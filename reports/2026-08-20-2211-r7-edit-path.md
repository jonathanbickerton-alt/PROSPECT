## 2. Edit seeding

`handleEditStart` recognises `churnMode` and seeds the panel: the stored target,
the control to churn, the ramp off — a row that reaches row-edit is single, by
the bar below.

**The comment at the site now carries three modes.** It already said restoring
`amountType` and `percentageBasis` is *not optional*, because a percentage event
reopened without them saves back as absolute and its amount changes meaning from
ten per cent to ten subscribers. Churn is the same shape one turn further: a row
reopened without its statement is a bare volume, and saving that volume writes it
back through the path that negates it.

### The mount refused my first seeding, correctly

I seeded through `applyAmountControl` — the one writer, which felt right. **It
refused churn**: the writer validates the arm against the *current* draft, and on
edit the draft's scenario arrives in the *same batch*. The writer saw the old
scenario, declined, and the panel stayed shut.

**A restore is not a transition.** It is the state arriving whole, which is
exactly what the transition writer exists to police for *user actions* and has no
business policing here. The stored control is now set directly, and exclusivity
is untouched: there is still one stored value, and the derived control reports
churn only once the restored scenario is Outflow.

## 3. Save Changes re-states

The churn branch in `handleSaveEdit` re-runs the emitter against the current
series and rebuilds the row: the delta and all four stated fields **re-snapshot
together**. A freshly stated target beside a stale delta would be a row whose
figures came from two different moments — the mixed-axes defect at this card.

**Its position closes the inversion structurally.** Below it, `neg` forces an
Outflow amount negative — right for a hand-typed magnitude, catastrophic for a
delta whose sign carries direction. The churn draft never reaches that line
rather than being excused by it, which is the same move fix B made on the add
path.

### The second defect the mount found: re-stating against your own effect

The first version ran the fold against `marketEvents` **including the row being
edited**. So the row re-stated against a series its own delta had already moved:
the churn it had reduced read as the current rate, and a fresh target of the same
size derived a delta of **zero**.

The pricing card has had `eventScopeSeriesFor(draft, excludeId)` for exactly this
reason and I did not carry it across. The series now excludes `editingEventId` —
and on Add there is nothing to exclude, because a draft has no id.

## 4. The member bar

A row of a multi-row churn campaign declines row-edit with a named reason, in six
locales. Its target is **cumulative**: month 2 of a 1/3/6 ramp states six points
against a base the first two months already moved, so alone it means nothing and
editing it desyncs the member from its siblings.

**A rule at the entry point**, not a guard downstream — `handleEditStart` is the
only place a single row can be opened, so it is the only place the refusal can
fire. The `:512` campaign bar answers a *different* question: it refuses to
reverse-engineer a ramp from summed volumes. Two bars, two questions.

The refusal is **feedback, not a disabled button** — a row that silently ignores
a click reads as a bug.

## 5. The mount extensions

**128 → 151 checks.**

**(a) Reopening seeds**: the panel renders, the stored target is shown rather
than a default, the breakdown renders against the current series, the ramp is

**(b) Save re-states, and the sign survives.** The decisive figure:

```
target    0.5pt -> 1pt
prevBase  12,501,786.25 -> 12,501,786.25   (unchanged — the exclusion works)
```

The ratio check is the one that discriminates: doubling the statement on an
unchanged base must exactly double the delta, and a stale or re-derived-elsewhere
figure would not.

**(c)** a three-month ramp exists, a member declines with its reason and the
panel does not open for it. **(d)** a plain volume event still opens for edit and
does not open the churn panel — the regression guard.

### The third defect: my own test inputs

The first (b) run compared a 2pt statement with a 3pt one and got **identical**
deltas. The fixture's annualised churn at that slice is **~1.73%**, so both
targets floor the rate to zero and the delta saturates at the whole outflow.
**That behaviour is correct** — `spec:churn-fold` already asserts the floor — and
my inputs were simply outside the range where the check means anything. Moved to
0.5 and 1.0.
